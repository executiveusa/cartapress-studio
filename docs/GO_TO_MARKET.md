# CartaPress Studio — Founder launch plan / 2026-11-10

## 0. Operating decision

**Mode:** SELL first, with a small shared studio and a bounded print referral experiment.

**Business objective:** Validate paid demand for custom menu design in 30 days, not spend 30 days building another generic menu SaaS. Target at least three paid orders, >=$400 collected service revenue, >=80% delivery on-time, <=1 correction round caused by avoidable layout/content errors per order. These are targets, not forecasts. Reassess on day 14 if no orders.

**Revenue offer:** fixed-scope menu layout work for independent restaurants, cafés, taquerías, food trucks, bars and caterers. **Shared platform:** CartaPress SvelteKit design and export engine. **Bounded experiment:** partner-linked printing, activate only after first fulfilled orders and tracked referrals. Defer QR hosting, automated social posting, AI photography, subscriptions, full Canva-like drag-and-drop, and POS integrations until service demand validates.

## 1. Positioning

**Name:** CartaPress Studio — working brand only. Domain and trademarks not cleared, no domain purchased.

**One-liner:** “A menu that looks like your restaurant—designed for the table, the phone and the printer.”

**Unique mechanism:** The customer uses an attributed carousel of 190 menu references to identify tone; the agent translates the brief into an *original* menu and produces a repeatable handoff with verified prices, editable vector/source files, and printer proof. The promise is a controlled workflow, not copying screenshots or inventing food photography.

**Ideal first buyer:** Independent, owner-operated restaurant with 10–60 menu items and an outdated Word/PDF menu; no brand agency; time-sensitive changes; competent at answering a short checklist and confirming prices. Reject projects with unsafe licensing, unrealistic two-hour print guarantees, or complex 30-page multilingual catalogs as initial gigs.

**Customer problems solved:** unclear menus that are hard to read, outdated prices, expensive designer revisions, unprintable files, inconsistent print/QR presentation, and the owner having to re-explain requirements.

## 3. Offer + risk reversal

Milestone 1—**Menu facts confirmed**: customer signs off on dishes, descriptions, prices, names, allergens as provided, and page count.

Milestone 2—**Design direction approved**: customer chooses concept and sees accurate first proof; edit rounds limited by package.

Milestone 3—**Export approved**: owner approves final digital proof; studio exports files; print vendor independently confirms preflight before physical order.

**Guarantee to publish:** “We make the approved menu match your supplied menu details and agreed brief. If we deliver a file with an error caused by our work, report it within 14 days and we'll correct that error at no extra design fee. Printer charges, changed prices, new content and third-party production defects are outside the correction promise.” Respect each marketplace's refund and dispute rules.

No claims about increased order value, sales, new customers, conversions or guaranteed revenue without an actual controlled customer study.

## 4. Order intake

Customer provides: restaurant name, business type, full approved menu text or legible source, authoritative price list, branding/logos they have rights to use, language and menu currency, desired print size/quantity, address/URL (optional for QR), first design references, deadline, print provider requirements (paper, trim, bleed, CMYK ICC profile) and any legally required ingredient/allergen statements.

Agent returns a structured editable brief with source links and a list of unanswered questions. If information missing, the draft is *blocked*, not invented. Keep client personal data out of design prompts unless essential and contractually permitted.

## 5. Agent delivery protocol (ICM)

Intake agent: parse menu sections/items, normalize currency, preserve source strings, list ambiguity and possible typos.

Creative direction agent: map selected visual references to abstract attributes (hierarchy, contrast, typography, palette, spacing). **Never reproduce artist designs, logos or proprietary illustrations.** Propose original style from five defined themes.

Production agent: use MCP to render SVG/PDF, ZIP and proof; check print safe areas and overflow warnings; generate QR only for client-controlled URL.

QA reviewer (independent): compare *every dish and price* against authoritative client content; verify readability at actual size, color contrast, image licensing, margins, spelling, notes/allergens, page count, export file opening; record PASS/FAIL evidence.

Delivery agent: send *only* client-approved files through marketplace-approved channels. Do not fabricate portfolio examples or claim human-only design. Capture feedback and correction tickets in the job record. Print coordination and changes require client approval.

**Hard stop:** any change to a price, legal claim, allergen notice, live hosting, payment, messaging or physical print order without external approval.

## 6. Thirty-day go-to-market

### Days 1–3 — prove the sample
- Build 3 original demo portfolios using fictional names and content (e.g. bistro, taquería, coffee shop) clearly marked concept work. Show mobile/desktop, close-ups, before/after only when an actual licensed before exists.
- Test download ZIP, PDF, SVG in Adobe/Inkscape; physically print one A4 proof and compare 100% text accuracy. Get independent designer preflight. Fix blockers before accepting orders.
- Publish Fiverr gig and Upwork Project Catalog only after honest, complete profile verification and all platform requirements. No account impersonation, no bot-created reviews.

### Days 4–7 — controlled launch
- Publish one narrowly targeted Fiverr gig and one Upwork Project Catalog offer. Keep packages consistent. Use relevant tags: restaurant menu design, cafe menu, food truck menu, print ready menu, bilingual menu.
- Run 10 **individually qualified** Upwork job searches (not spam blasts); send 3–5 tailored proposals/day only where work fits and platform policies allow. Respond to genuine buyer inquiries promptly.
- Test two thumbnail variations on listings; do not claim reviews or experience that does not exist.

### Days 8–14 — collect first sale and evidence
- Track views, clicks, conversations, proposals, qualified leads, paid orders, accepted orders, delivery hours, corrections and net contribution margin. Split by Fiverr vs Upwork.
- Goal: 1 paid completed order and a documented client testimonial (only if genuinely given, with permission). If zero inquiries after >=100 organic listing impressions, change lead image/title. If inquiries but no conversions, revise scope/price, not the software.

### Days 15–21 — improve throughput
- Collect repeat errors, add validation checks and preflight tests. Make three original sample cases from completed work only with buyer permission.
- Offer an optional $29/month update package to buyers who have already received the design. Do not create unsolicited subscriptions.
- Apply to MustHaveMenus / MOO / vetted reseller programs. Use ordinary print provider links until accepted.

### Days 22–30 — decide
- Goal: 3 paid orders, >=$400 gross service receipts, >=80% on-time delivery, <=1 preventable revision loop/order, >=3 qualified leads in pipeline.
- If unmet: keep as bounded service experiment; fix offer or choose different buyer segment. If met: continue and productize job intake. Do not ship a standalone SaaS subscription without demand proof.

## 7. Customer acquisition channels

**Priority 1 Fiverr:** high-intent keywords, three original gig images, service details, FAQ, realistic 3–6 day scope. No portfolio artwork that belongs to inspiration authors.

**Priority 2 Upwork:** Project Catalog plus proposals only for real publicly posted menu/menu-redesign needs. Relevant examples, mention deliberate AI-assisted workflow and client accountability.

**Priority 3 direct:** personalized audit of a restaurant's *public* menu (no scraping private data), a 3-point improvement suggestion, and a respectful invitation to view a demo. Verify outreach law and channel policies first. Never send high-volume unsolicited messages.

**Referral path:** independent print shops, restaurant suppliers, restaurant site agencies, POS installers; revenue-sharing only after written partner agreement and platform compliance. Avoid moving Fiverr/Upwork transactions off-platform in violation of terms.

## 8. Printer/affiliate research, with actual state

- **MustHaveMenus:** [public affiliate page](https://www.musthavemenus.com/content/affiliate) advertises 10% recurring revenue on qualifying new clients including print or subscription purchases. Eligibility, last click, payment timing and link API require enrollment and approval.
- **MOO:** [partner page](https://www.moo.com/us/partner) offers affiliate/reseller opportunities, up to 12% for new-customer orders (terms apply). Check that selected products are appropriate for restaurant menus.
- **Newprint:** [partner program](https://www.newprint.com/partner) describes 3–5% affiliate commissions and reseller routes, subject to approval and geographic service.
- **FedEx Office:** [restaurant menu printing](https://www.office.fedex.com/default/menus.html) accepts uploaded artwork for actual menus, sizes 4x9, 8.5x11 and 11x17 inches at the time of research; no verified affiliate program.
- **Mexico:** [TuMenuDigital](https://tumenudigital.mx/partners) has digital menu referral terms, not a physical print solution. Optional future digital recurring affiliate experiment, not core now.

**Rule:** don't display an affiliate badge, claim commissions earned, assert a referral partnership, use tracking URLs or automatically send files to a printer before approval and technical integration. Add affiliate disclosures adjacent to recommendations once links are tracked. Printer PDF may need ICC-managed CMYK workflow; do not send our current RGB proof without print vendor confirmation.

## 9. Product roadmap with acceptance gates

**Slice 0 (included in archive):** 190-link catalog source, five original themes, editor, deterministic export, initial MCP, pricing page and draft gig copy. Acceptance: can load 190 live, selecting refs persists in one brief, all formats download, QA rejects missing price, responsive layout. Only pure tests have run; install/build and browser tests remain.

**Slice 1 (next):** source metadata snapshot in repo to eliminate upstream dependency, image licensing review or consent, preflight (overflow, text and font embedding), actual printer sample, deployment and live smoke tests. Acceptance: independent reviewer signs off on physical proof; 0 pricing mismatches in a 60-item test.

**Slice 2:** durable client workspaces with authentication and scoped sharing, client approvals, checkout in app for direct orders, official marketplace integration only if documented and platform-approved. Acceptance: paid test order → verified invoice → downloadable approved files.

**Slice 3:** automatic print quote/order integration through approved print partner; affiliate tracking and reporting. Acceptance: 1 legitimate attribution visible in partner dashboard and paid commission or documented expected payout, not a click counter.

**Slice 4 (park):** true CMYK ICC pipeline, arbitrary multi-size/bifold templates, image ingest and license ledger, multi-user collaboration, subscriptions, language service and live hosted QR menus.
